Prioritise articles focused on AI and machine learning research, engineering, and applications — including model releases, technical breakthroughs, industry developments, new tools or frameworks, and policy or safety discussions directly related to AI/ML systems. Articles from technical blogs, research labs, arXiv, and reputable tech outlets covering these topics should rank highly.

Give additional weight to content about agentic AI systems, including evaluations of small or open-weight models on tool-use and reasoning tasks, production patterns for deploying AI agents, and security or capability considerations in agent architectures. Practitioner-level insights covering real-world implementation patterns such as least-privilege scoping, gating strategies, or eval-driven testing are valuable alongside formal research. First-person practitioner accounts describing concrete multi-agent architectures — such as role decomposition, orchestration models, or task delegation strategies — should be prioritised when they offer replicable or generalisable design patterns.

Place particular emphasis on articles addressing the gap between prototype and production AI systems — challenges at scale, failure modes, operational complexity, reliability engineering, and lessons from real deployments. Security vulnerability analyses are especially valuable when grounded in concrete CVEs or recurring structural failure patterns such as unsafe code execution, insufficient sandboxing, or missing input validation.

Also prioritise substantive expert interviews and opinion pieces that critically examine foundational assumptions about AI and ML systems, provided they demonstrate technical depth and go beyond surface-level commentary.

Filter out low-signal social media exchanges, replies, and informal posts — including short conversational fragments, single-sentence acknowledgements, congratulatory messages, personal shoutouts, joke responses, promotional name-reservation prompts, or fleeting mentions of AI topics with no analytical substance. Bare URLs accompanied by minimal or no explanatory text should be excluded regardless of destination. Content that is purely social or promotional in nature, even if posted by known figures in the field, should be excluded entirely. A minimum threshold of coherent, self-contained technical or analytical content must be met before any article is considered, regardless of topic relevance.
