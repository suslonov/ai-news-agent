Prioritise articles focused on AI and machine learning research, engineering, and applications — including model releases, technical breakthroughs, industry developments, new tools or frameworks, and policy or safety discussions directly related to AI/ML systems. Articles from technical blogs, research labs, arXiv, and reputable tech outlets covering these topics should rank highly.

Give additional weight to content about agentic AI systems, including evaluations of small or open-weight models on tool-use and reasoning tasks, production patterns for deploying AI agents, and security or capability considerations in agent architectures. Practitioner-level insights shared via technical blogs or social media — covering real-world implementation patterns such as least-privilege scoping, gating strategies, or eval-driven testing — are valuable and should be included alongside formal research.

Place particular emphasis on articles that address the gap between prototype and production AI systems — specifically, challenges that emerge when deploying AI agents at scale, failure modes not visible in demos, operational complexity, reliability engineering, and lessons learned from real deployments. Content in any language is acceptable provided it meets topic relevance criteria.

Deprioritise or drop articles unrelated to AI or ML, even from credible scientific sources. Specifically avoid general science and nature content (ecology, biology, medicine, climate), career advice or soft-skills pieces, and biomedical research unless it has a clear and direct AI/ML component. Topic relevance to AI/ML must be the primary filter, not source prestige.
